Part 1

The 7-point Decision Friction Model

Seven points where a reader's decision to click breaks down. Each one is scored independently. Tap any point to see it on a real subject line.

01

Filing Label Subject

▾

The subject line names a topic category instead of a reader consequence.

Before
"Q1 Product Update"
After
"Your reports just got 3 hours faster"
02

Feature-First Lead

▾

The opening line announces what the product does before it answers what changes for the reader.

Before
"We've launched Advanced Reporting Dashboard."
After
"Your reporting just cut 3 hours from the weekly review."
03

Zero / Buried Social Proof

▾

The strongest stat exists somewhere in the email, just not where the reader is still paying attention.

Before
Proof buried in paragraph 4
After
"4M users cut overspend by 23% in month 1" — sentence 2
04

Missing Visual Hierarchy

▾

Every feature gets equal size, equal spacing, equal weight. Nothing tells the eye which claim matters most.

Before
8 features, identical formatting
After
1 dominant claim, everything else secondary
05

Implied Transformation

▾

Adjectives stand in for numbers. Adjectives don't convert, concrete before/after does.

Before
"Faster reporting."
After
"From 4 hours to 40 minutes."
06

Consequence-After-Caveat

▾

The real reason to care shows up after a qualifier or apology has already lowered the reader's attention.

Before
"Unfortunately due to changes... but here's why it matters"
After
Consequence stated first, context second
07

Guest Language CTA

▾

The reader is positioned as a visitor to the brand's offer, not the owner of an action. Highest fail rate of all seven checks.

Before
"Learn more"
After
"Fix my reporting"
One of these six patterns shows up in 96% of every email we've scored.↓
Part 2

The 6 failure patterns, ranked by frequency

Across 59 real SaaS emails audited, these six patterns account for almost every point lost. Different industries, different company sizes, same handful of structural bugs.

Guest Language CTA96%

The single highest fail rate of all seven checks. Fix: ownership language — "Fix my reporting," not "Learn more."

Feature-First Bias83%

Fix: state the reader's outcome first, name the feature that produces it second.

Filing Label Subject83%

Fix: replace the topic label with a reader consequence.

Implied Transformation74%

Fix: swap adjectives for a concrete before/after number.

Missing Visual Hierarchy71%

Fix: one claim gets dominant visual weight, everything else is secondary.

Zero / Buried Social Proof69%

Fix: move one specific proof signal into the first scroll.
