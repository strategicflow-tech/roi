Part 1

The 7-point Decision Friction Model

Seven points where a reader's decision to click breaks down. Each one is scored independently, 1 to 10 total.

01

Filing Label Subject

The subject line names a topic category instead of a reader consequence. "Q1 Product Update" tells the reader what folder this belongs in, not why opening it changes anything.

02

Feature-First Lead

The opening line announces what the product does before it answers what changes for the reader. The reader's actual question sits unanswered below the fold.

03

Zero / Buried Social Proof

The strongest stat or customer result exists somewhere in the email, just not where the reader is still paying attention. Most readers have left by paragraph two.

04

Missing Visual Hierarchy

Every feature gets equal size, equal spacing, equal weight. Nothing tells the eye which single claim matters most.

05

Implied Transformation

Adjectives stand in for numbers. "Faster reporting" instead of "from 4 hours to 40 minutes." Adjectives don't convert, concrete before/after does.

06

Consequence-After-Caveat

The real reason to care shows up after a qualifier, an apology, or a caveat has already lowered the reader's attention.

07

Guest Language CTA

"Learn more." "Explore now." "See what's new." The reader is positioned as a visitor to the brand's offer, not the owner of an action.

Part 2

The 6 failure patterns, ranked by frequency

Across 59 real SaaS emails audited, these six patterns account for almost every point lost. Different industries, different company sizes, same handful of structural bugs.

Guest Language CTA96%

The single highest fail rate of all seven checks. Fix: ownership language — "Fix my reporting," not "Learn more."

Feature-First Bias83%

Fix: state the reader's outcome first, name the feature that produces it second.

Filing Label Subject83%

Fix: replace the topic label with a reader consequence — "Your reports just got 3 hours faster."

Implied Transformation74%

Fix: swap adjectives for a concrete before/after number.

Missing Visual Hierarchy71%

Fix: one claim gets dominant visual weight, everything else is secondary.

Zero / Buried Social Proof69%

Fix: move one specific proof signal — a number, a name, a result — into the first scroll.
