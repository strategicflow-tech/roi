SaaS Directory Approval Times in 2026: Full Comparison Table (DR, Dofollow Status, Days to Live)

Most SaaS founders submit to directories in one batch and assume the backlinks go live together. They don't. The gap between the fastest and slowest directories on this list is three weeks — and during that gap, your slower submissions are sitting in a queue generating zero SEO value. This article presents a structured comparison of 10 directories across four variables: approval time, domain rating (DR), dofollow status, and submission model. Use it to sequence your submissions correctly.

Methodology

How this data was gathered
  • Approval times: Based on direct product submissions made by the ToolIndex team between March and August 2026, cross-referenced with community reports on X, Indie Hackers, and relevant Slack groups (n = 40+ submissions per directory for the major platforms).
  • Domain Rating (DR): Pulled from Ahrefs as of August 2026. DR is a logarithmic 0–100 score representing the strength of a domain's backlink profile. Values are approximations and fluctuate ±2–5 points. See note under table.
  • Dofollow status: Verified by inspecting the rel attribute on outbound links from each directory's listing pages using browser DevTools. Status applies to the primary product URL link on the listing page; secondary links may differ.
  • Scope: Directories included are those with a DR of 35 or above and an active, publicly submittable listing format as of August 2026. Review aggregators (G2, Capterra, GetApp) are excluded from this analysis because their backlink model is fundamentally different from directories.

Definitions

Domain Rating (DR) is Ahrefs' measure of how authoritative a domain's backlink profile is, on a logarithmic scale from 0 to 100. A DR 86 domain is not "twice as authoritative" as DR 43 — it is orders of magnitude more powerful, because the scale is non-linear. Moving from DR 70 to DR 80 requires more cumulative link equity than moving from DR 0 to DR 70.

Dofollow backlink means the link passes PageRank — it transfers link equity from the directory's domain to your product's domain, contributing to your domain authority and search rankings. A nofollow backlink carries the rel="nofollow" attribute, which instructs Google not to pass link equity. Nofollow links can still drive referral traffic and brand visibility, but they have no direct impact on your domain's ranking power.

Approval time is measured from the moment the submission form is submitted to the moment the listing URL is publicly accessible and the backlink is live. For directories with editorial queues, times represent the median observed, not the maximum.

What the Table Actually Shows

Three patterns emerge from the data that are counter-intuitive for most founders:

1. Speed and DR are not inversely correlated. The naive assumption is that instant-approval directories are low-quality link farms, while slow-approval directories are high-authority. The data contradicts this. ToolIndex has the highest DR in the table at 86 and approves in under 60 seconds. BetaList takes 1–3 weeks and has a DR of 73. SaaSHub takes 24–48h and is nofollow at DR 69. Editorial review does not generate domain authority — it reflects an editorial philosophy, nothing more.

2. Dofollow correlates more with business model than with approval speed. The four directories that give dofollow backlinks (ToolIndex, Peerlist, Fazier, FoundrList, AlternativeTo) are not a homogeneous group — they span the full range from instant to 3-day approval. The nofollow directories (SaaSHub, StackShare, Futurepedia, BetaList) are similarly distributed across the approval speed spectrum. Whether a directory uses dofollow or nofollow is a policy decision tied to their monetization model and Google relationship, not their quality.

3. The highest-value submission combination in 2026 is ToolIndex + AlternativeTo. ToolIndex: DR 86, dofollow, instant. AlternativeTo: DR 80, dofollow, 1–3 days. Both give link equity. Together they represent 166 combined DR points of dofollow coverage — and both can be submitted in under 10 minutes. No other two-directory combination on this table matches that output.

Why Approval Time Matters for Launch Sequencing

Directory backlinks do not generate ranking signals the moment the listing goes live — they generate signals when Google crawls the directory page containing your link. High-authority directories are crawled more frequently, but there is still a crawl delay of anywhere from hours to days. This means the actual SEO benefit of a backlink lags behind the listing going live by a variable amount.

The practical implication: every day a directory submission sits in a review queue is a day your domain is not receiving that link's crawl signal. For a pre-launch period of 3–4 weeks, sequencing matters enormously. Submit slow directories on day one of pre-launch preparation, not on launch day.

Takeaway: The correct sequencing rule is: submit in reverse order of approval time, starting with the slowest. BetaList goes in first (week −3). AlternativeTo and StackShare go in at week −2. SaaSHub at week −1. ToolIndex, Peerlist, Fazier, and FoundrList go in on launch day — they are live in under 24 hours and give you dofollow coverage active before your first press coverage arrives.

Common Reasons Submissions Stall or Get Rejected

Even directories with automated or light review can stall submissions for predictable reasons:

  • Broken or redirecting URL: The most common rejection trigger across all directories. Always verify your product URL resolves correctly before submitting. Redirect chains (especially 301 → 302 → final URL) can cause automated checks to fail.
  • Generic description: Descriptions that read like taglines ("an AI-powered platform for teams") are flagged by editorial reviewers as insufficient. Write a functional description: what the product does, who it does it for, and what the primary action is.
  • Product not yet publicly accessible: Directories that require a live product (AlternativeTo, SaaSHub, StackShare) will reject pre-launch applications. For pre-launch products, use BetaList specifically — it is designed for this stage.
  • Duplicate listing: If your product already exists under a different name, domain, or variant URL, a new submission is flagged as a duplicate. Check whether a listing already exists before submitting — claim or edit it instead of creating a new one.
  • Category mismatch: Submissions without a clear category match may sit in a manual review queue indefinitely. Always select the most specific applicable category rather than a broad parent category.
