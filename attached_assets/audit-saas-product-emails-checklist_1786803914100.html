How to Audit SaaS Product Emails for Structural and Conversion Issues (7-Point Checklist)

By Alex Iliescu, founder of Strategic Flow · 15 Aug 2026 · 7 min read

Opens are fine. Clicks aren't. That gap almost never gets fixed by better subject lines, it gets fixed by finding where the structure breaks.

Most SaaS teams treat a weak email the same way: rewrite the subject line, tweak the button color, ship it again. That fixes almost nothing when the real problem is structural, not cosmetic.

This is the audit framework we use before touching a single word of copy: what to check, in what order, and which failure pattern each check is designed to catch.

Quick answer

Direct answer

If opens are fine but clicks are weak, the problem is almost always structural, not deliverability. A structural audit checks subject framing, lead framing, proof placement, and CTA language. A deliverability audit checks SPF, DKIM, DMARC, and inbox placement. They diagnose different failures and neither substitutes for the other.

Structural audit vs deliverability audit

A structural audit asks whether the email is persuasive once it's opened. A deliverability audit asks whether it reaches the inbox at all. These are different failure modes with different symptoms.

Structural problems show up as decent open rates paired with poor click-through. Deliverability problems show up as low inbox placement, high bounce rates, or failed authentication checks. Running a deliverability scan on a structurally broken email won't find the real issue, and rewriting copy on an email that never reaches the inbox won't move a single metric.

The 7-point audit checklist

Run this on any onboarding, trial, or promo email before assuming the problem is deliverability or that the list is fatigued.

  1. Subject framing. Does it announce a result, or just a category? "Your setup finishes in 2 minutes" beats "Getting started with X."
  2. Lead framing. Do the first two lines talk about the reader's problem, or about your feature?
  3. Proof placement. Is the strongest proof point (a number, an example, a result) near the top, or buried at the end?
  4. Claim framing. Is each claim written as a benefit to the reader, or as a list of capabilities?
  5. Visual hierarchy. If someone reads only the first sentence of each paragraph, do they still get the point?
  6. CTA language. Does the button use ownership language ("Claim your audit"), or generic invitation language ("Learn more")?
  7. Decision friction. After opening the email, how many decisions does the reader have to make before acting? Every extra one is friction to remove.

A lean audit stack

One tool per layer is enough. A deliverability checker for inbox health, a lifecycle audit for journey-level logic, and a message-architecture audit for the actual conversion path inside a single email. That combination covers the three most common failure points without duplicating work.

FAQ

What's the difference between a structural audit and a deliverability audit?
A structural audit checks whether an opened email is persuasive enough to get clicked. A deliverability audit checks whether the email reaches the inbox at all. They catch different failures and neither one substitutes for the other.
My open rates are fine but clicks are low, what should I check first?
Start with a structural audit: subject framing, lead framing, proof placement, and CTA language. Low clicks despite healthy opens is almost always a structural problem, not a deliverability one.
Should I audit one email or the whole lifecycle?
If a single email underperforms, audit that email structurally. If onboarding, trial, and retention emails all underperform together, the problem is likely sequence logic, which needs a lifecycle audit instead.
What counts as decision friction in an email?
Any extra step the reader has to take before acting: an unclear CTA, competing links, a claim that needs more context to trust, or a subject line that doesn't match the ask inside.
