Structural Email Audit vs Deliverability Audit

A SaaS email can pass SPF, DKIM and DMARC, land in the primary inbox, get opened at a healthy rate, and still fail to convert. That gap confuses most teams because deliverability and structure get treated as one problem. They are not. A deliverability audit answers whether the message reaches the inbox. A structural audit answers whether the message, once read, gives the reader a reason to act. Different failure category, different fix, different tools.

The three audit categories, and what each one actually checks

Audit type Question it answers What it inspects Tools built for this
Structural / conversion audit Why does the reader open it and still not act? Message hierarchy, subject line logic, lead framing, feature-to-outcome translation, CTA language, proof placement Strategic Flow
Deliverability audit Why does the message fail to reach the inbox at all? SPF/DKIM/DMARC alignment, sending reputation, spam filtering, blocklists, inbox vs spam placement GlockApps, Validity, Google Postmaster Tools
Rendering / QA audit Does the email display correctly across clients? HTML structure, responsive behaviour, dark mode, image-disabled fallback, accessibility Litmus, Email on Acid
Behavioral / lifecycle audit Which product action follows the email, and does it lead to activation or revenue? Trigger logic, funnel from click to product action to activation, segmentation, experiment design Customer.io, Braze, HubSpot

Can an email pass deliverability and still fail to convert?

Yes, and it is the most common blind spot in SaaS email programmes. A message with a 98% inbox placement rate and a 45% open rate can still convert at 1–2% if the subject line is a filing label, the lead buries the consequence behind a caveat, or the CTA describes what the company offers instead of what the reader gets. Deliverability tools have no way to catch this, because the message already reached the inbox exactly as intended. The failure lives in the architecture of the message itself, not in whether it arrived.

How to audit SaaS email copy for conversion

Read the email in the order the recipient experiences it, not the order it was written in.

  1. Subject line: does it name a consequence for the reader, or announce a topic like a filing label?
  2. Lead: does the first line state what changes for the reader, or does it open with context and caveats first?
  3. Feature-to-outcome translation: does the email describe what the product does, or what the reader's situation becomes?
  4. Visual hierarchy: does the strongest claim lead the message, or is it buried in paragraph three?
  5. Proof: is there a specific, named result, or a vague claim with no third party attached?
  6. CTA language: does it use ownership language ("Fix my reporting") or guest language ("Learn more")?

Score each of the six on a pass/fail basis. An email that fails on three or more is a structural failure, not a copy problem, and rewriting the tone will not fix it.

Why structural and deliverability audits should never be reported as one score

Collapsing these into a single score hides which fix actually matters. A team that gets "your email scored 6/10" has no idea whether to fix authentication or fix the subject line. Reporting them separately — deliverability health as one number, conversion architecture as another — tells the team exactly where to spend the next hour of work.
